1. why this tool

When a problem feels stuck, it's often because we're acting on the visible symptoms instead of the deeper system producing them. fathom helps you map a problem as a system and pick where to intervene.

It uses the iceberg model — four layers that sit on top of each other, with leverage growing as you go deeper.

2. the four layers

events
the visible, day-to-day things people notice. "a heatwave killed five people last week."
patterns
what's happened repeatedly across events. "summer mortality has crept up every year for a decade."
structures
the policies, incentives and resource flows producing the patterns. "zoning has reduced tree cover in low-income suburbs."
mental models
the beliefs and assumptions holding the structures in place. "air conditioning is a luxury, not a right."

3. drawing your system

  1. Pick the pencil. Click anywhere to draw a node, then drag from one node to another to create an arrow.
  2. Click an arrow to flip its polarity — + means more of A leads to more of B, − means more of A leads to less of B.
  3. Click a node to open the right-hand panel. Set its iceberg layer using the four-button picker.
  4. The accent ring around the node shows its layer at a glance.

4. reading the connections

Each node shows two small numbers underneath:

  • → N in — how many things flow into this node. High values usually mean a symptom: lots of stuff feeds it.
  • N out → — how many things flow out. High values mean a lever: changing this node ripples widely through the system.

A node with high out-degree at a deep iceberg layer (structures, mental models) is usually where you'll get the most leverage for the least effort. Use this to narrow down where to act.

5. simulating

Hit play at the bottom. Hover over a node and click the up/down arrows to push more or less of it into the system. Watch how the change propagates through the arrows you drew. Use this to test your assumptions before committing to an intervention.

6. start with an example

The examples in the top bar give you three pre-mapped systems (environmental, health, social) so you can see the iceberg layers in action. Load one, play with it, then build your own.
